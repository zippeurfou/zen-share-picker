# Design

This is how zen-share-picker works and why it is built this way. The README covers install and use. This page is for people who want to check the reasoning or change the code.

Line numbers in code comments point at the Zen 1.24.2b sources. Comments and tests also cite decisions as "P13" or "R32". Those numbers are resolved in the decision log at the end.

## Problem

When a site asks to share the screen, Zen shows Firefox's standard permission prompt. It is a small panel at the top left, next to the address bar. Its dropdown lists the shareable sources, tabs included, by title, one line each. You see one entry at a time, and a preview only of the entry you already picked. With a dozen tabs, finding the right one is slow, and on a video call everyone waits while you do it.

Spaces add a second problem. Many people keep work and personal tabs in separate spaces so that they never mix on screen. Zen's prompt lists the tabs of each window's active space. With a second window on another space, the dropdown shows that space's titles too, in the middle of a presentation.

Once the share runs, Firefox shows its sharing bar: a small, always-on-top window at the top center of the call's window, with a short label and a Stop Sharing button. To get it out of the way you drag it or minimize it to the Dock. Zen doesn't restyle it.

## Goals

* When a site asks to share the screen and Zen's prompt lists tabs, screens or windows, a picker opens in the window: a search box, a list of tabs with icon, title and host, and a large preview of the highlighted tab.
* The current space's tabs come first, most recently used first. The tab used just before the one asking to share starts highlighted.
* Other spaces' tabs are hidden until the user presses Tab. Once shown, they sit under their space's name with a "careful" mark, and sharing one takes a second Enter.
* A Windows tab in the picker lists the screens and windows Zen's prompt lists, never Zen's own windows, with a live preview of the highlighted one. Where Zen's prompt has an operating-system entry, the Windows tab also has a row that hands over to the operating system's own picker.
* Keyboard first: type to filter, arrows to move, Enter to share, Esc to decline, Cmd+1 and Cmd+2 (Ctrl elsewhere) to switch between Tabs and Windows.
* Nothing is ever shared without an explicit Enter or a click on Share.
* It never breaks sharing. If Zen's prompt is not what the add-on expects, the picker stays away and the prompt works as if the add-on were not installed.
* During every share in Zen, Firefox's sharing bar is a larger, rounded pill at the bottom center of the call's page, still draggable, with a label that names the share type and the site, and a Hide button next to Stop sharing. If the restyle can't apply, the stock bar stays.
* about:addons shows the add-on's own icon instead of the default puzzle piece.

## Non-goals

* A grid of thumbnails.
* Capture types Zen doesn't offer. If the prompt lists no tab, screen or window, the add-on has nothing to show.
* Sharing a whole app, and app names or icons for windows. Zen lists neither apps nor app names.
* A preview for every window. One live preview runs, of the highlighted row.
* Turning Apple's picker off at runtime. Only the installer's opt-in `--list-windows` flag does that.
* Changing how sites request sharing, or any site's own interface.
* Regular Firefox and other Firefox-based browsers.
* A listing on addons.mozilla.org. The add-on is privileged and unsigned.
* Replacing Firefox's sharing bar, changing what Stop sharing does, restyling the bar's microphone and camera toggles, translating its new labels, or a settings switch for the restyle.

It must also never touch the address bar, never write a pref at runtime, never store a title, URL or image, never press anything in the prompt other than its real Allow button, and never set a permission, not even for its live preview.

## How it works

### Entries and mapping

Zen opens the prompt as the `webRTC-shareDevices` notification. Its dropdown (`#webRTC-selectWindow-menupopup`) holds one item per source, each with a label, a `mediaSource` and a `deviceId` (`WebRTCParent.sys.mjs`, `listScreenShareDevices`). A tab entry has `mediaSource` "browser", and its `deviceId` is the tab's browser id as a string. Firefox builds the tab entries from `BrowserWindowTracker.getAllVisibleTabs()`, which in Zen means each window's `gBrowser.visibleTabs`: the tabs of that window's active space, plus the Essentials Zen shows there (P13).

Every time Zen shows the prompt, the add-on reads it (`src/glue/prompt.sys.mjs`). It skips the separator and the "Select a window or screen" placeholder, and checks every other item. Entries are told apart by kind and id, since a tab's browser id can equal a screen's or a window's id. A screen entry has `mediaSource` "screen" and a window entry "window"; their labels are Zen's ("Entire screen", or the window's title). A camera or microphone request, a screen request that also asks for a microphone or a tab's audio (P34), or a prompt with neither a tab entry nor a screen or window row (P52), is left alone with no record. Anything else unexpected is a problem code for that prompt.

The add-on then maps each tab entry back to its tab, in any window and any space, through `browser.browserId` (`src/glue/tabs.sys.mjs`). From the tab it reads the title, the host, the favicon, the last-accessed time, the Zen space and the container. If one entry doesn't map to a tab, the picker stays away: it could not tell which space that tab belongs to, so it could not keep it hidden.

### Which tabs count as here

"Here" is the requesting window's active space and that space's container (`hereOf`).

* A regular or pinned tab belongs to its Zen space.
* An Essential belongs to every space whose container matches the Essential's container, which is where Zen shows it. An Essential from another container counts as another space.
* A tab whose space or container can't be read counts as another space. So does every tab when the requesting window has no space, as in a private window.

Other-space tabs are grouped by space, with Essentials of other containers in their own group, and the groups are ordered by their most recent tab.

### Mirrored tabs

Zen's window sync can show one logical tab in several windows. Firefox then lists it once per window. The picker shows one row per logical tab, keyed by the synced tab's DOM id (P9). It keeps the copy that holds the live content, otherwise the requesting window's copy, otherwise the most recently used one (P8). Sharing a copy without content would show a blank page.

### Screens and windows

What Zen lists depends on the platform and on one pref. On macOS, `media.getdisplaymedia.screencapturekit.picker.enabled` is on by default: Zen hands screens and windows to Apple's picker, and its prompt holds only the operating-system entry ("Use operating system settings"). With the pref off, which the installer's opt-in `--list-windows` flag sets in the profile (P54), Zen lists "Entire screen" and every on-screen window by title, and no operating-system entry. Zen reads that pref only at startup. The add-on reads no pref to decide what to list: the Windows tab follows whatever the prompt lists (P52).

`captureRows` (`src/lib/captures.mjs`) turns the entries into rows: screens first, then windows, each in Zen's order, with no re-sorting by recent use. The operating-system entry is never a row, even where Zen lists it as a screen. A window row's title is Zen's label; Zen exposes no app name, process or icon.

Zen's own windows are never rows (P50). A Zen window's title is the title of its active tab, which can belong to another space, so listing it would show that title before Tab. The add-on reads `windowUtils.webrtcRawDeviceId` of every top-level Zen window (`src/glue/windows.sys.mjs`), the id Zen's window entries carry, and hides the entries that match. Zen lists the request's windows again each time the prompt shows, so an id seen as Zen's at any read of a prompt stays hidden for that prompt, even after that window closed (P70). If any read throws, as it does headless, it hides every window entry of that prompt and keeps the screens, and records the problem code `zen-ids-unknown` once for that prompt when it lists a window. The debug log counts the hidden entries.

### The picker

The picker (`src/glue/overlay.sys.mjs`, with its rules in `src/lib/state.mjs` and `src/lib/rows.mjs`) is an HTML overlay in the window's `#zen-main-app-wrapper`. It sits in the middle of the window: Zen's prompt is hidden while the picker is open (P35), so nothing covers it. Its header names the site asking, as Zen's prompt did, set as text only: "call.example wants to see your screen", with the delegation and local-file wordings of Zen's own prompt (P37). Share takes Zen's accent color, `--zen-primary-color`, and looks disabled until the picker is armed; Cancel is neutral. One picker per window at a time.

Above the list, a strip holds two tabs, Tabs and Windows. A switch clears the search, any pending second Enter and the Windows highlight; the Tabs highlight stays, or goes back to the first tab when a search had cleared it. The Windows tab exists only when it has rows. The picker opens on Tabs, or on Windows when the prompt has no tab row; Tabs then says there are no tabs to share here.

Rows are built only from what is visible. Before Tab, other spaces' rows are not in the DOM at all: no title, host, icon, device id or preview. The hint "Tab: show tabs from your other spaces" appears only when another window shows another space, and has no count. Search filters by title and host and never reveals. The requesting tab comes after the current space's other tabs and is never highlighted at the start.

The Windows tab holds the screen and window rows, then the operating-system row when the prompt has an operating-system entry, the one Zen pre-selects (P17, P52). That row reads "Open the macOS picker (apps, windows or a screen)" on macOS and "Open the system picker (apps, windows or a screen)" elsewhere, and maps only to that entry (P6). A search filters the screen and window rows by title and never hides the operating-system row, which is the way out when Zen lists no window. The Windows tab opens with nothing highlighted. A highlighted screen row shows the hint "Careful: everything on this screen will be visible.", in place of the warning Zen's hidden prompt shows for a screen (P72).

The picker owns a few keys: the arrows, Home, End, Enter, Esc and Tab. Tab reveals other spaces in Tabs and does nothing in Windows. Shift+Tab is swallowed and does nothing (P10). Accel+1 and Accel+2 (Cmd on macOS, Ctrl elsewhere) switch to Tabs and to Windows, wherever focus is in the window, and move focus to the search box; the picker swallows them even when there is no Windows tab (P53). Every other key with Alt, Ctrl or Cmd passes through to Zen. Other keys go to the search box. A press on a row only highlights it, and a press on Share or Cancel keeps focus in the search box (P22).

Enter and Share commit a choice (a tab, a screen, a window or the operating-system row) only once the picker is armed: `security.notification_enable_delay` must have passed since it opened or since its window was last activated, as for Zen's own Allow button. Until then they change nothing and Share is disabled. A held-down Enter (an auto-repeat) and an untrusted event never commit. `api.js` checks the same delay on its own before it acts on a share (P33).

The picker closes without sharing when the stock panel hides, when the user switches tabs or spaces (also when the switch keeps the same tab selected, through Zen's space-switch listeners), when the prompt is replaced, when a listed tab is closed or put to sleep (P11), and when the picker is turned off. Each of these ends the hiding of Zen's panel and declines nothing. Focus loss closes nothing and shares nothing, and neither does a switch between the picker's own Tabs and Windows.

### Preview

Both previews live in `src/glue/preview.sys.mjs`.

For a tab, the preview is a still snapshot of the highlighted row's tab, taken with `PageThumbs.captureTabPreviewThumbnail`, the helper behind Firefox's tab hover preview. It refreshes about once a second while the row stays highlighted. One capture runs at a time, and a result for a tab that is no longer highlighted is dropped. Nothing is captured until a row is highlighted, and an other-space tab only after Tab revealed it. The snapshots stop while the Windows tab shows.

For a screen or a window, the preview is live, through Firefox's `<webrtc-preview>` element, as Zen's own prompt previews its selected entry (P51). There is one, of the highlighted row only. The picker feeds every state change through `liveTarget(model, state)`, which names the highlighted screen or window only while the Windows tab shows and that row is visible, and null otherwise. So the capture starts only when an arrow key, Home, End or a click highlights a screen or window row, never on opening, on a switch or from typing, and it stops at once when the highlight moves or hides, the view switches to Tabs, a re-read prompt drops the row, the picker closes or a share starts. A new target starts only once the highlight has rested on its row for 150 ms (P71): Firefox can't cancel a capture once requested, so holding an arrow key across the list must request none. A new target stops the old capture before it starts, and a start that resolves late is stopped, so there are never two attached streams. The element is created on the first target, never before.

The live preview is off, and nothing is created, when `media.getdisplaymedia.previews.enabled` is false, as in Zen's prompt, or on macOS when the screen-recording permission reads as denied. The add-on only reads that state and never requests it. It writes no permission for the preview either: the system principal the preview runs under already reads as allowed, and Zen's own grant before its preview stores nothing for it. Until a frame arrives (a video width above 0), the area stays blank for a short wait and then says "No preview"; after a failed start it says so at once. A refused capture is not retried until the highlight changes.

A live capture of a tab exists in the code as a fallback mode for the tab preview, but the snapshot mode is the one in use.

### The share sequence and its verification

The add-on never grants a share. It drives Zen's prompt the way a user would, and Firefox keeps every permission check. `runShare` (`src/lib/share.mjs`) is the only code that presses Allow, and only after an Enter or a click on Share:

1. It stops the picker's preview, so that only one capture ever runs (P19).
2. It selects the chosen item in the stock dropdown and dispatches the item's `command` event, as a real pick does. Zen then runs its own checks and starts its own preview. The add-on reads the selection back. A tab target must be a tab entry, a window or screen target an entry of that kind whose row the picker listed, and the operating-system target the recorded entry (P19). On any mismatch it puts the old selection back and stops, with nothing pressed.
3. It closes the picker. Zen's panel stays hidden (P35) and keeps working, because Zen's Allow check looks at whether the window is active and at the delay, not at visibility (platform test S6a).
4. It waits until the window is active, Allow is enabled, and `security.notification_enable_delay` has passed since the panel was shown. Firefox ignores an earlier click on purpose, as a guard against tricked clicks, and that delay restarts when the window activates.
5. It presses the real Allow button (`button.click()`), right after checking in the same step that the prompt and the selection are unchanged. A click Firefox ignores gets one retry.
6. It verifies: the notification is gone, and Firefox's list of active shares shows, for the requesting tab, a device of the chosen kind and id: a tab (`browser`), a window or a screen. As soon as the prompt is no longer current (gone after the click, replaced or hidden), Zen's panel shows again, before this check starts (P43).

The outcomes are `shared`, `handed-off` (the operating-system picker took over), `assist`, `aborted` and `unverified`. If Allow doesn't take within about 5 seconds, the outcome is `assist`: the chosen tab, window or screen stays selected in Zen's prompt, with Zen's preview showing, and the user presses Allow. Nothing ever runs a share again for that prompt, and a throw after a click is `unverified`, never `assist`, so the add-on never asks for a second Allow (P18). Whatever the outcome, the hiding ends when the sequence settles, at the latest.

The add-on never calls `mainAction.callback`, `_onButtonEvent` or the `webrtc:Allow` message, never presses the prompt's secondary button or its menu, and never touches the prompt's checkbox. It calls `PopupNotifications.remove` only to decline.

### Declining

Esc and Cancel decline the request (P36). `runDecline` (`src/lib/decline.mjs`) checks that the prompt is still the session's own and, in the same step, abandons it once with `PopupNotifications.remove(notification, true)`, Zen's path for a request removed without a user response: WebRTC denies the request and sets no permission (P38). It checks at once that the notification went, shows Zen's panel again, and waits up to a second for the notification to go. The outcome is `declined`. If the call throws or the notification stays, Zen's prompt shows again, the problem code is `decline-failed`, the outcome is `cancelled`, and the user decides in Zen's prompt. A held-down Esc and an untrusted Esc or Cancel do nothing; arming does not gate a decline, which is not a commit. No other close declines: a tab or space switch, a closed or sleeping tab, a navigation, the off switch and shutdown leave the request pending.

Why not press Zen's secondary button, as a user would? Its label and its effect depend on the site. It reads "Not now" only when the site already has a camera or microphone allowed or active. Otherwise it reads "Block", which sets a temporary block on the site. A ticked checkbox in the prompt turns either into a remembered decision. The button also needs the window to be active. Abandoning the request avoids all of that: it presses no button, reads no checkbox, needs no active window, and sets no permission on any site (platform test S7, and acceptance tests with and without a camera grant).

While Zen's panel is hidden, the add-on owns Esc and keyboard focus in that window (P44), since the user cannot see what a key would act on there. Zen has its own Esc handler on the window: with focus on the page or in the panel, it presses the top notification's secondary button, which on a site without a camera grant is "Block". So the hiding element comes with two guards, added and removed with it (`src/glue/conceal.sys.mjs`). A capturing `keydown` listener on the window cancels every Escape, which also stops the keypress Zen listens to. While the picker is open, the Escape goes to the picker as its own Esc, wherever focus is, and declines as above; during the Allow wait or a decline it does nothing. A capturing `focusin` listener on the panel sends focus that enters it to the picker's search box, or to the page once the picker closed, so F6, Tab, Space or Enter never reach the unseen Allow or Block. The same `keydown` listener also cancels every chord of the chrome access-key modifiers (`ui.key.chromeAccess`: Ctrl on macOS, Alt elsewhere) with a key that is an access key in the panel, wherever focus is, since such a chord would press Allow or Block (P47). Gecko handles access keys on the keypress, and sends no keypress for a cancelled keydown.

A prompt the add-on handed back, after a failed decline or a share, gets no picker again when Zen shows it again.

### Fail-safe

At start, in the first browser window, the add-on checks Zen's notification panel, the prompt's elements, Firefox's list of active shares, the Zen space API and the thumbnail helper (`src/glue/selfcheck.sys.mjs`). If one fails, or a module doesn't load, the add-on is `inert`: it opens no picker and listens to no prompt, tab or space. It still adds one `unload` listener per window, to forget the window when it closes (`src/glue/watch.sys.mjs`).

Each prompt is checked again when it shows. Any failure there, including a reason code the wiring doesn't know and a throw, means that prompt gets no picker. The code goes to the status (the last 10) and to the browser console. The stock prompt is left exactly as Zen built it, and in view: a prompt with no picker is never hidden, and a failure to hide it is the code `conceal-failed` with no picker (P35). The hiding starts as the panel starts to show, for a prompt the picker will take (P48), and ends at once if the picker then does not open, or after 3 seconds at the latest with the problem code `hide-timeout` (P64). A prompt whose panel opened without the add-on's `nonnative` gets no picker and no hiding; the debug log says `prompt shown-native` (P64). While the panel is hidden, Zen's sidebar and toolbar stay as they were; a prompt shown again opens them as stock Zen does (P67).

The hiding is one style element per window, carrying the add-on's marker and owned by the picker's session. Every way a session ends removes it, except a share, which removes it once its prompt is no longer current or its sequence settles. A sweep runs on top of that, independent of any session: turning the picker off, shutdown (so uninstall and the old version in an update in place) and each window's own cleanup remove every marked element, and a window the add-on starts watching is swept first. The marker is the same in every version, so the sweep also removes an older version's leftovers.

The options page shows the state, the failed checks, the problem codes, the sharing bar's problem codes, the last outcome, an off switch and the debug log. The off switch and the debug switch last until Zen restarts, because the add-on stores nothing (P27). Each version loads its modules from its own resource host, so an update in place runs the new code (R16).

### The sharing bar

During a share, Firefox opens its global sharing indicator, a separate always-on-top window (`webrtcIndicator.xhtml`, opened by `webrtcUI.sys.mjs`). If anything but webrtcUI closes that window, every screen share stops, so the add-on never closes it and never hides it by closing. It restyles the window in place (`src/glue/bar.sys.mjs`, with the pure parts in `src/lib/bar.mjs` and the styles in `src/glue/bar.css`). Firefox still decides when the bar shows, and Stop sharing still runs Firefox's own code. The restyle runs whenever the add-on's modules loaded, whatever the picker's state: the off switch, an `inert` picker and failed startup checks leave it on (P76). It runs on macOS only in 0.3.0; on Linux and Windows the bar stays stock (P86).

**Restyle in place.** The add-on watches for indicator windows, open at start or opened later. At the bar's `DOMContentLoaded`, before Firefox's `load` handler sizes and shows it, it loads its sheet as an author sheet through `windowUtils`, since the bar's content security policy blocks a style element (P77). Every rule sits under the root's `data-zen-share-picker-bar`, so a sheet left behind changes nothing. The add-on hides Firefox's device toggles, separators and minimize button, moves the grip first, adds one label element and a Hide button, and gives Stop sharing its sentence-case text. The colors come from Zen's accent pref, or the system's `AccentColor` when that pref is the default, with contrast floors (P84). `BAR_SHAPE` and `BAR_HIDE_OPACITY` in `src/lib/config.mjs` are ready fallbacks for square window corners and a visible re-hide (P82).

**The update hook.** Firefox rewrites the bar on every change, through the window's `updateIndicatorState`. The add-on wraps that function: the wrapper runs Firefox's update first and in full, then refreshes the label, the colors and the position, and catches its own errors. Firefox also updates the bar from its own `load` and `sizemodechange` handlers, which bypass the wrapper, so the add-on listens to both, after Firefox's handlers (P77).

**Placement.** After every update, and on tab switches and resizes of the call's window, the bar sits at the bottom center of the content area of the window holding the latest share, the browser Firefox itself centers on. Its bottom edge sits 88px above the content's bottom when that share's tab is the selected one, which clears the toolbars of the usual call sites, and 16px otherwise (P73). Split view is not detected, so a call visible in a split but not selected gets 16px (P83). The bar stays draggable. Firefox's own updates move the bar too, for example to clamp it onto a screen, so a position change alone can't tell a user's drag. A press on the bar outside its buttons arms it, until 250 ms after the mouseup or until the add-on next moves the bar. A move with the press armed, outside Firefox's updates and the add-on's own moves, is a drag. After a drag the add-on never moves that bar again (P79).

**Hide.** Hide does what Firefox's own `setVisibility(false)` does, and remembers the shares running at that moment, keyed by the requesting browser and the kind of share (P74, P78). Firefox shows the bar again on every update, so right after each update, in the same task, the add-on hides it again, before a frame can show it. It lets the bar show only when a share it doesn't remember started and Firefox's update just made the bar visible, so it never shows a bar `privacy.webrtc.hideGlobalIndicator` keeps hidden. Shares that ended are forgotten on every update, so a share that stops and starts again counts as new. When every share ends, Firefox closes the bar, and the next share opens a fresh, visible one. A hidden share stays stoppable from the macOS menu bar item, which keeps a hidden bar hidden, from Zen's site panel and from the site.

**Labels.** The label names the share type and the requesting page's host, in English, and never a title: "Sharing a tab with {site}", "Sharing a Zen window with {site}", "Sharing a window with {site}" and "Sharing your entire screen with {site}" (P75). With several shares the kind follows Firefox's order: the screen, a Zen window, a tab, then another window. `{site}` is the host of an `http:` or `https:` page only. With several sharing sites, or none with a host, the site is dropped. The label carries an `aria-label` with the full sentence, and the verb phrase truncates before the site (P81).

**Fallback.** Off macOS, and with `privacy.webrtc.globalMuteToggles` on, the add-on leaves the bar stock, by choice: the debug log says `bar stock platform` or `bar stock mute-toggles`. Markup it doesn't know, a restyle that throws and a later update that throws all leave the stock bar, visible as Firefox decides, and add a `barProblems` code to the status: `bar-unknown-markup`, `bar-style-failed` or `bar-internal`, the last 10 kept (P80).

**Shutdown.** Disabling, removing or updating the add-on, and quitting, give back a stock bar: Firefox's sheet only, its own elements and texts, its own `updateIndicatorState`, then one Firefox update, so a bar the add-on hid shows again unless Firefox's prefs hide it. A bar the add-on moved and the user never dragged goes back to Firefox's top-center spot first. Firefox stops re-centering a bar that anything else moved, and nothing outside its indicator can reset that, so for the rest of that share the stock bar no longer re-centers on later updates (P79). When the bar itself is unloading, the add-on gives it back without that Firefox update: Firefox's own `unload` handler has already removed the bar's menu bar items, and an update could add them back. Leftovers of an earlier version, its marker, elements, sheet and wrapper, are cleared before a restyle.

### Privacy

* Titles, hosts, icons and snapshots live only in memory, while the picker is open, except that the opt-in debug log keeps hosts until it is turned off. Window titles live only in the picker's DOM: no file, console line, status or debug line holds one.
* No frame of a live preview is copied, drawn or saved.
* The add-on writes no prefs and no permissions, and stores nothing. The `--list-windows` pref is written by the installer, on the user's request, never by the add-on.
* Console lines carry reason codes only, never a title, URL, host or image.
* The sharing bar's label holds the share type and the requesting page's host, never a tab or window title. Its added elements have no tooltip.
* The debug log is off by default, in memory, capped at 2000 lines, and emptied when turned off. It holds entry and tab counts, the hosts of current-space rows and of rows revealed with Tab, device ids, the counts of screens, windows and hidden windows, outcome and problem codes, and the sharing bar's lines: `bar placed 88` and `bar placed 16`, `bar hidden N shares` with N a count, `bar dragged`, `bar shown new-share`, `bar stock mute-toggles`, `bar stock platform` and `bar hide refused`. It never holds a title (of a tab or a window) or an image, and another space's host only after Tab (P12).

## Approaches considered

**How to share.** Three options:

1. Drive Zen's own prompt: read its entries, show the picker, then select the same entry in the stock dropdown and press the stock Allow button. Chosen. Firefox keeps every check and grants the share itself.
2. Replace the prompt and grant the share directly. Rejected. It means copying Firefox's permission code from `WebRTCParent.sys.mjs`, which is security-sensitive and can change in any release, and the module does not export the function that builds the prompt.
3. Restyle the prompt with CSS only. Rejected. CSS can make the panel wider, but it can't add search, a preview that follows the highlight, or hidden spaces.

**Pressing Allow, or leaving it to the user.** The add-on could stop after selecting the tab (an assist mode) and let the user press Allow. Tests showed that Zen accepts a click on the real Allow button once its delay has passed and the window is active, also in a headless window. So the add-on presses it, and falls back to assist when the click doesn't take.

**Layout.** A grid of thumbnails, or a search list with one large preview. The list won: it is faster from the keyboard and fits many tabs.

**Other spaces.** Always listed but dimmed, hidden until asked, or listed with a confirm step. Hidden until asked won, with a second Enter for a revealed tab on top. A dimmed row still shows its title on screen.

**Preview.** A live capture of the highlighted tab, as Zen's own prompt does for the selected entry, or a still snapshot. Snapshots won for tabs. They need no capture permission, they are cheap enough to refresh once a second, and they never leave a second capture running next to the real share. Windows and screens have no snapshot helper, so their preview is live, one at a time, and only for a row the user highlighted.

**Windows on macOS.** Three options:

1. A Windows tab that only hands over to Apple's picker. It stays as the fallback: without `--list-windows`, that row is all the Windows tab holds on macOS.
2. A thumbnail for every window. Rejected: one capture per window is heavy, and each can still raise the macOS alert.
3. Zen's own window list, with Apple's picker turned off by an opt-in installer flag, and one live preview of the highlighted row. Chosen. The cost is plain: no Apple picker in Zen, no whole-app sharing, titles only, and, on macOS 15 and later, an alert that can ask whether Zen may bypass the system private window picker, at the first window capture (maybe a screen one too) and again later. For other apps it comes back about every 30 to 90 days. That is why the flag is opt-in and never set at runtime.

**Zen's own windows.** Listed like any other, or hidden. Hidden won: a Zen window shows its active tab's title, which can belong to another space, and a tab row is the better way to share Zen content anyway. When the ids can't be read, every window is hidden rather than guessed at.

**The sharing bar.** Four options:

1. Restyle Firefox's bar in place. Chosen. When the bar's window opens, the add-on adds a sheet and a few elements, keeps the window at the bottom center and adds Hide. Firefox still decides when the bar shows and what Stop sharing does, and its macOS menu bar item keeps working. If the restyle can't apply, the stock bar stays.
2. Our own bar, with Firefox's turned off through `privacy.webrtc.hideGlobalIndicator`. Rejected. It allows any look, but the add-on would have to rebuild stopping a share, and a broken or disabled add-on would leave no floating bar at all.
3. A bar inside the Zen window. Rejected. It disappears whenever another app is in front, which is when you need it most.
4. Hiding the bar by pref. Rejected. No code, but it gives up the bar instead of making it better.

**Packaging.** A feature inside zen-space-scope, or its own add-on. Its own add-on won: a picker that breaks after a Zen update can't affect anything else, and anyone can install it alone. It reuses zen-space-scope's installer, update channel and test harness.

## Testing

* Unit tests in Node cover the row model (space rules, mirrored tabs, order), the state machine (keys, search, reveal, the second Enter), the share sequence and the decline sequence with a fake prompt and clock, the picker's header copy for a site, a delegated request and a local file, the arming gate, the startup checks, the screen and window rows (Zen's windows hidden, every window hidden when the ids are unknown) and the live preview's target and its one-capture rule.
* Install tests run the installer against fake profile trees, never a real one.
* Release tests check the update manifest script, the workflows, and what the built add-on file holds: only the add-on's own folders, and the icons the manifest names.
* Acceptance tests drive a real Zen through Marionette, each in a fresh temporary profile, with local test pages and no internet. They request a share from a page on a placeholder host (`meet.test`), use the picker by keyboard and mouse, press the real Allow button, and check the stream the page receives. Tests that need another space's tabs in the prompt open a second window on that space (P14). The checks that another space stays hidden come with a positive control on the same setup: after Tab, the same tab shows. Other acceptance tests check that Zen's panel is hidden while the picker is open and shows again on every exit, that a click where the hidden panel sits misses it, that the picker is centered, and that Esc and Cancel reject the page's request with no "screen" permission set, both for a site with a camera grant ("Not now") and without one ("Block"). Window rows are tested with fake windows: tab entries turned into window entries, with previews off, so no real window is captured. Tests that list the real windows read only keys, ids and counts, never a title.
* Unit tests also cover the sharing bar's pure parts in `src/lib/bar.mjs`: the label for every share type, the position, the accent colors and their contrast floors, the drag's 2px tolerance and which shares count as new after Hide. The bar's acceptance tests (`tests/acceptance/test_bar.py`) share a tab through Zen's stock prompt, which raises no macOS alert, and read computed sizes, colors, positions and the window's visibility in Firefox's real bar: the restyle, the label, the whole-screen state set on a live bar, Hide and a new share bringing the bar back, placement and drags, both prefs, the fallback, shutdown, and that no title shows. They run headless, on macOS: each waits up to 10 s for the bar, and a missing bar fails the test. Off macOS they skip, except one that checks the bar stays stock (P85, P86).
* Opt-in capture tests (`tests/acceptance/test_capture.py`) preview and share a real window and the screen. They can raise the macOS alert, so they run only when `ZSP_CAPTURE_TESTS=1` is set, headful, on macOS, with someone there to answer it.
* CI runs all of it on every push and once a day against the latest Zen release, the acceptance tests on headless Linux (P29).
